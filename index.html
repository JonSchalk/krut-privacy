Krut — Privacy Policy

Effective 23 September 2026

Krut collects nothing. There are no accounts, no servers, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Nothing you photograph or record leaves your iPhone unless you deliberately send it.

Krut is a range logbook and target scorer for iPhone. It photographs a paper target, finds the holes in it and scores them. All of that happens on your device.

What Krut stores, and where

On your iPhone, and nowhere else:

  • your sessions and series, and the ranges, firearms and target faces you name
  • each shot's position, as a millimetre coordinate on the target sheet
  • the photographs you take of your targets
  • your settings

All of it lives in the app's own storage. Deleting Krut deletes all of it.

Your phone's own backup

If you back up your phone — to iCloud or a computer on an iPhone, to your Google account on Android — that backup can include Krut's data, as it can every app's. The backup goes to your own account, under Apple's or Google's protection; Krut does not send it, cannot read it there, and adds nothing to it. Turning your phone's backup off keeps Krut's data on the phone alone.

Camera and photo library

  • Camera — to photograph a paper target so shots can be detected and scored. The photograph is processed on the device.
  • Photo library, reading — only when you choose an existing photograph to score.
  • Photo library, adding — only if you turn on saving a copy of each target photograph.

No image is uploaded anywhere.

The three ways data can leave, all by your hand

  • Game Center ranking — optional, and off unless you turn it on. It submits two numbers, both your Krut score, 1–100, computed from your average per shot and your grouping: one for your shooting as a whole, and one for the range day just finished. They go to two leaderboards because a leaderboard keeps your best submission, so a single board would rank a best day against a lifetime. No photographs, no shot positions, no session details. They go to Apple's Game Center under your Apple Account and are then governed by Apple's privacy policy.
  • Party mode — optional, and nothing is advertised or sent until you create or join a party and tap send. It is the largest of the three, and the only one where what leaves goes straight to another shooter's phone rather than to somewhere you picked: at a range, one phone keeps the scoreboard and the others send their sessions to it over Bluetooth. Phone to phone — no network, no server, no account, and nothing passes through us.

    What crosses is your display name; each series and its shots, as millimetres on the target; and the session's date, range, distance and firearm.

    What does not: no photographs, and no photograph filenames; not your own notes on the session; and nothing about how the detector read the picture. That list is not a promise made in prose — one function in the app builds what leaves, field by field, and a test pins the exact set. A field added to a session stays on your phone unless it is deliberately added to that function.

    The phone keeping the scoreboard holds the figures it computed — each shooter's name, series count, shots, total, average and grouping — and does not keep the shot positions once the party is over. Anyone in earshot of a party being hosted can see its name, the range, the distance, the target and the series size before joining; that is how a shooter finds the right party, and it is all a phone that has not joined can read.

    The scoreboard comes back. The phone keeping score sends it to every phone that has joined, so while you are in a party the other shooters' display names and figures — their series and shot counts, total, average, grouping and Krut score, and when each of them last sent a series — reach your phone, and yours reach theirs. That is what a scoreboard is, and it is the only thing that travels in that direction. Nobody else's shot positions come back, nor their photographs, notes or firearm: the scoreboard is built field by field from the figures alone, and a test pins that set the same way it pins what leaves. The board you see is the one from your last connection — a phone in a party does not hold an open link — so it is stamped with the time it was made.

  • A card you share — optional, and nothing is made until you tap share. A session card draws that session's shots where they landed on the target face, with the date, the range, the firearm and its calibre; a stats card draws your figures over the span you are looking at. It is a picture, and it goes wherever you send it — a message, a post, a file — so once you have sent it, where it goes next is that app's business and not Krut's. Nothing is sent anywhere until you choose a destination, and Krut never sees one.

What Apple collects

If you install Krut through TestFlight or the App Store, Apple may collect crash and performance data and installation statistics. That is Apple's collection, not Krut's, and it is governed by Apple's own privacy policy. Krut contains no crash reporter, no analytics library and no third-party code that reports anything.

What Google collects

If you install Krut through Google Play, Google may collect crash and performance reports and installation statistics, and show them to the developer, as it does for every app it distributes. That is Google's collection, not Krut's, and it is governed by Google's own privacy policy. Krut adds nothing to it.

Third parties

None. Krut has no third-party SDKs, no advertising, no attribution and no payment processing.

Children

Krut collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the effective date above changes with it. Because Krut collects nothing, any change would be to widen what the app can do — and a version that sent anything new would say so in the app before it did.
